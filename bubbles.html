Supermarket Champagne for Christmas

You don't need a famous name on the label to get a good bottle of Champagne. The Telegraph's Christmas taste test tried the supermarkets' own-label Champagnes blind, then scored each one for taste and for value. Nearly all of them did well. Below is our summary of the results, with prices we checked on 24 November 2026.

One tip from the test: the risky bottles tend to be the obscure brands sold at "half price", not the supermarkets' own labels. If one of the picks below comes on offer, that's the time to stock up.

The top four

Sainsbury's Taste the Difference Brut NV

£24.25 · buy 6+, save 25% with Nectar until 15 Dec

Warm and biscuity, thanks to a big share of Pinot Meunier, but it stays lively rather than heavy. With the Nectar six-bottle deal, each bottle works out at about £18.

Sainsbury's →

Tesco Finest Premier Cru Brut NV

£26.00 · £22.00 with Clubcard until 31 Dec

A lighter, Chardonnay-led style with a gentle bready, toasty edge. The tester thought it wasn't quite as good as in past years, and found it opened up after being poured for a while. Tesco's buy 6+, save 25% deal also applies until 30 November.

Tesco →

Co-op Les Pionniers Brut NV

Best Buy · £29.50

A long-standing favourite among own-label Champagnes: fresh, crisp and appley, with more depth than you'd notice at first sip. It's gone up a lot in price over the last couple of years, but it's still highly rated.

Co-op →

Waitrose Champagne Brut NV

Best Buy & Best Value · £26.00

The overall winner. It's mostly Pinot Noir, so it's fuller and a little savoury, with red-fruit and toasty notes, rounded off by some Chardonnay. A good choice if you like a richer style. Not available in Northern Ireland, as there are no Waitrose shops here. Worth picking up on a trip to Great Britain.
